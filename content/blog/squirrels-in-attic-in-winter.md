Squirrels don't leave an attic in winter. They don't hibernate, they don't migrate, and a warm attic is the best den they'll find all year. If you're hearing them in December, expect to hear them in February too, and by March there may be babies. Winter is a good time to get them out, and here's why.

## Do squirrels hibernate?

No. Grey squirrels stay active through the whole winter. Ontario's Royal Botanical Gardens says tree squirrels don't hibernate, and that they shelter in the nest through freezing or damp weather and come out when it eases. Earth Rangers, the Canadian kids' conservation group, says the same thing in plainer words: eastern grey squirrels are active all year round.

What changes is how much you notice them. On a bitter day a squirrel may not leave the nest at all. On a mild one it's out at first light, digging up what it buried in the fall. So the noise overhead comes and goes with the weather, and a quiet week in a cold snap doesn't mean they've moved out.

## Why your attic, and why now

In the wild, a grey squirrel winters in a hollow tree if it can get one. Hinterland Who's Who, the federal wildlife series, notes that in winter several squirrels may share the same tree den. Hollow trees are scarce in a city. An attic is a very large hollow tree with the heat on.

That sharing habit matters for you. The squirrel you saw go in at the roof edge may not be living alone up there. More bodies means more droppings, more flattened insulation and more chewing.

Most winter squirrels moved in during the fall, when they were building winter nests. People often first notice in December or January only because the windows are shut, the house is quiet and the furnace isn't running all the time yet.

## What they're doing up there all winter

- Sleeping in a nest hollowed out of your insulation, usually near the hole.
- Going out to feed on milder days and coming back by the same route. Look for tracks in the snow on the roof or along a fence leading to the house.
- Gnawing. Their front teeth keep growing, so they chew wood, vent screens and cable all winter. Our post on [squirrels chewing wires](/blog/squirrels-chewing-wires-in-attic) covers why that one is a fire risk.
- Widening the hole. A gap that was a tight squeeze in October is often a comfortable doorway by spring.

The hole itself costs you too. It lets heated air out and cold air, snow and meltwater in, and the insulation they've packed down isn't doing its job.

## What happens if I wait for spring?

Babies happen. Grey squirrels mate in the middle of winter. Illinois's wildlife agency puts mating in January, and the City of Mississauga puts the first litter of the year in March and April. A female that's wintering in your attic is very likely to have her young there.

Once there are babies, the job changes. They can't walk out through a one-way door, so they have to be found and lifted out by hand first. It can still be done, and we do it. It's just a longer job than the same attic in January.

Waiting also doesn't end with the squirrels leaving. The young raised in your attic know the way in, and squirrels have a second litter in summer.

## Can squirrels be removed in winter?

Yes, and because they don't hibernate, the usual method works. A one-way door goes over the hole. The squirrels push out to feed and can't push back in. In a deep freeze they may sit tight for a few days first, so the door stays on until they're out.

People sometimes worry that it's cruel to put a squirrel out in the cold. A grey squirrel is built for an Ontario winter, with a thick coat and food buried all over the neighbourhood, and each one knows several places to shelter. What it loses is the easiest den on the street. It doesn't lose its chance of getting through the winter.

What you shouldn't do is seal the hole yourself on a day when it's quiet. Quiet in January usually means they're asleep inside. A squirrel shut in will chew its way out through something else, and that something may be a ceiling.

## Does the City's method work in the cold?

The City of Toronto suggests making the attic unpleasant with a bright light and a talk radio station, then checking whether they've gone. It works sometimes. In winter it has less going for it, because the squirrel's other choice is the cold, and it will put up with a lot of radio to stay warm. And when it does work, the hole is still there.

## Signs to look for in winter

- Running, scratching or rolling sounds overhead, mostly in the morning and late afternoon, and more on mild days.
- Tracks in fresh snow on the roof, the eavestrough or the top of a fence.
- A patch of roof edge where the snow has melted or been knocked away around a gap.
- Bits of insulation or chewed wood on the ground below the roofline.
- A squirrel carrying leaves or paper up to the roof.

None of these is certain by itself, and seeing none doesn't mean the attic is empty.
