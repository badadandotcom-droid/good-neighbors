If there are baby raccoons in the attic, the mother has to stay with them until they can leave together. In Ontario most kits are born in spring, and they spend about eight weeks in the den before they're big enough to go out with her. Close the hole or take the mother away before then, and the babies are left behind. Here's how to tell whether there are babies up there, and how they come out safely.

## When raccoons have babies in Ontario

Raccoons mate in late winter, mostly in March. Canada's Hinterland Who's Who fact sheets put the peak of births in May, and the City of Toronto's guide to the city's mammals says the same. A few litters come earlier or later, so a mother in the attic is possible from early spring into late summer.

The kits stay in the den for about eight weeks. After that they start going out with their mother. The City's guide has Toronto's young raccoons leaving the den in July.

The City notes that from early spring into mid-summer, raccoons are looking for warm, safe places to have and raise their young. An attic is warm, dry and high off the ground, which suits a mother raccoon well.

## Signs there are babies up there

You can't always tell from below, but a few things point to a mother with kits:

- High-pitched chirping, chattering or whimpering, often from one spot. Our post on [what animal is in your attic](/blog/what-animal-is-in-my-attic) explains how that sound differs from birds.
- An adult raccoon going in and out of the same hole again and again, with sounds overhead that carry on after she's gone out.
- It's spring or early summer.

No sound at all doesn't rule them out. That's why we look before anything goes on the hole, whatever time of year it is.

## Why the mother has to stay

When the mother is trapped and taken away, the kits are usually left in the attic. Toronto Wildlife Centre and the City of Toronto both warn about this: babies left without their mother starve.

It goes wrong the other way too. Seal the hole while the mother is out, and the kits are stuck inside without her. A mother cut off from her kits will tear her way back in, often through a new part of the roof.

The only good outcome is the whole family leaving together. Once the kits can follow her out, the mother can move them to another den, and the hole can be sealed behind them.

## Baby raccoons in a chimney

Mother raccoons also have litters in chimneys. Ontario's advice is that waiting is often the easiest answer: after eight to ten weeks, the mother and her young leave and don't come back. Adults can climb out of a flue, but young kits can't, so the mother has to be free to come and go.

Don't light a fire to smoke them out, and don't drop mothballs down the chimney. Ontario and the Town of Newmarket both warn against it. Once the family is gone, a proper chimney cap keeps the next one out.

## Baby squirrels in the attic

A grey squirrel usually raises two litters in a year. The City of Mississauga lists births in March and April and again in July and August, and the City of Toronto's guide gives the same pattern, late winter and summer. A litter is often three to six babies.

A mother squirrel will defend her nest, so if she's chattering at you from the roof, back away and leave her be.

For squirrels, we use a reunion box. We find the nest, take the babies out by hand, and set them in a warm box outside next to the hole. That way the mother can carry them to another nest, and the hole can be closed.

## If you find a baby on the ground or in the house

A baby raccoon or squirrel on its own is often not an orphan. Ontario's advice is to leave young wild animals alone, because the parents are often close by and come back for them.

- Keep children and pets away and watch from a distance.
- Don't pick it up.
- If it's hurt, or you're not sure it has a mother nearby, call Toronto Wildlife Centre's hotline at 416-631-0662 for advice before you do anything else.

If a baby fell out of your attic, that's a sign there's a nest up there. Call us so the rest of the family can come out together.

## What not to do

- Don't close the hole, even with something temporary, until someone has checked for babies.
- Don't trap the mother and move her. Ontario law says a captured animal has to be let go within 1 km of where it was caught, within 24 hours, and moving her leaves the kits behind.
- Don't go into the attic to look while she's there. A mother raccoon with kits will defend them.
