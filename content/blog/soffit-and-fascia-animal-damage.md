The roof edge is where most animals get into a house. The soffit is the panel under the overhang, and the fascia is the board the eavestrough hangs on. A raccoon pushes a soffit panel up or pulls a soft fascia board away. A squirrel chews a hole at the corner. A patch over that hole lasts until the next animal tests it. A proper repair fixes what made the spot weak in the first place.

## Why animals pick the roof edge

Three things make this part of the house easy.

The first is the material. Soffit on many houses is thin aluminum or vinyl, held in a channel. It's made to keep weather out and let air through, not to stop an animal pushing on it.

The second is water. When an eavestrough clogs, water sits against the fascia and the edge of the roof boards. The City of Mississauga's wildlife advice tells homeowners to keep eavestroughs clear for that reason, and to replace rotted wood and fasten loose soffits. Soft wood is easy to chew and easy to pull.

The third is the shape of the roof. Where an upper roof meets a lower one, an animal can stand on the shingles and push up on the soffit above it. The City of Mississauga points to the same places: the eaves, soffits and fascia, especially at corners and where two parts of the roof meet.

## What the damage looks like

From the ground, look for:

- A soffit panel that has dropped, bent or slid out of line.
- A dark gap at a corner where the soffit meets the wall or the roof.
- Fascia that is chewed, split or pulled away from the roof edge.
- An eavestrough that sags or has come loose at one end.
- Insulation, leaves or nesting poking out from under the overhang.

Greasy rub marks and hair around the gap mean it's in regular use.

## Patch or repair

A patch covers the hole. That might be a piece of mesh screwed over the gap, a strip of metal, or the old panel pushed back up and fastened.

A patch is sometimes the right call. If the wood behind it is sound and the panel only came loose, fastening it properly with screws and backing it with steel mesh can be all it needs. The City of Toronto's advice for closing holes is along those lines: half-inch hardware cloth or sheet metal, reaching well past the edge of the hole on every side.

A patch is the wrong call when the wood is rotten. Screws don't hold in soft wood, and an animal that pulls on the patch takes the wood with it. Then you have a bigger hole and a patch on the lawn.

Spray foam is not a repair at all. A squirrel chews through it, and it gives a raccoon something to grab.

## What a proper repair involves

It starts with taking the damaged section apart far enough to see what's behind it.

- Rotted fascia is cut back to sound wood and a new board is fitted.
- The ends of the roof boards and rafters behind it are checked, and anything soft is replaced.
- The soffit is replaced or refitted, with steel mesh fastened behind it where an animal could push.
- The eavestrough goes back on, sloped so it drains.
- The new work is matched to the house in material and colour.

The cause gets fixed too. If a clogged or badly sloped eavestrough rotted the board, a new board with the same eavestrough will rot the same way.

## Don't block the vents

This is the part do-it-yourself patches often get wrong. Many soffits are vented. Air comes in through the soffit and leaves through the vents higher on the roof, and that flow keeps the attic dry.

The Asphalt Roofing Manufacturers Association describes it as intake at the eaves and exhaust near the ridge, and says the system needs both, unobstructed. Cover a vented soffit with solid sheet metal or fill it with foam, and the attic loses its intake. Over a few winters that can mean damp insulation and wet wood.

Steel mesh behind a vented panel keeps animals out and lets the air through.

## The animals come out first

None of this happens with an animal still inside. Close the roof edge on a raccoon or squirrel and it will open another spot, usually a few feet along the same soffit. Our post on what happens when you've [sealed an animal in your attic](/blog/sealed-animal-in-attic) goes through that.

From early spring into summer, there may be babies in the insulation just inside the gap. They're checked for before anything is closed.
