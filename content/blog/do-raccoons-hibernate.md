Raccoons don't hibernate. They den up, sleep through the coldest stretches and come out again when it warms up, sometimes weeks later. That's why a raccoon in the attic can go silent in January and be back to thumping around in February. If the noise stopped when the cold hit, it's most likely still up there.

## What raccoons do in winter

Hinterland Who's Who, the federal wildlife series, describes it as a period of inactivity and says plainly that it isn't hibernation. The raccoon's body temperature doesn't drop the way a groundhog's does. It just stays in the den and sleeps, and how much it moves depends on the air temperature outside.

It can do that because of the fall. A raccoon eats hard from September on and goes into winter carrying a lot of fat. Hinterland Who's Who says raccoons in the north live on that stored fat through the winter. Pennsylvania's Game Commission adds that they come out at different times during warm spells and are a good deal leaner by spring.

So the pattern in a Toronto winter usually looks like this:

- A cold snap: little or no sound for days, sometimes longer.
- A thaw: heavy walking overhead after dark, and the green bin knocked over.
- Late winter: more noise than at any point since the fall, for a reason covered below.

## Why an attic is the den they want

A raccoon in the woods winters in a hollow tree, a log or a burrow another animal dug. In the city, the dry, heated space under your roof is better than any of them. It's out of the wind, it's above the snow, and warmth leaks up into it from the house all night.

They don't need much of an opening to claim it. A loose soffit panel, a plastic roof vent or a soft fascia board at the roof edge is enough, and a raccoon is strong enough to finish the job. We wrote about [how raccoons get in the attic](/blog/how-do-raccoons-get-in-the-attic) and the usual spots.

## The noise stopped. Is it gone?

Probably not, if the weather turned cold at about the same time. A raccoon that's found a warm attic has no reason to give it up in the hardest part of the year.

A few things you can check without going on the roof:

- Fresh tracks in snow on the roof, the deck railing or the top of the fence after a mild night. Raccoon prints look like small hands.
- A dark, greasy rub mark or bent metal at one spot on the soffit.
- Snow melted or pushed away around a gap at the roof edge.
- Droppings in the same place on the roof or deck, added to after each thaw.

Seeing none of these doesn't prove the attic is empty. It only means the raccoon hasn't been out lately.

## Late winter is mating season

This is the part that turns a winter raccoon into a spring problem. Raccoons breed before winter is over. New Hampshire's wildlife agency gives January to March as the mating season, and a naturalist quoted by Northern Woodlands magazine says the first warm spell in February is usually when the tracks start to show up. Males travel a long way then, and they're noisy about it.

The kits come about two months later. Canada's Hinterland Who's Who puts the peak of births in May, with some earlier. Toronto Wildlife Centre's advice is to treat any raccoon in an attic from January on as a possible mother.

So a female that settles into your attic in December is very likely planning to have her young there in the spring. Once she has, the job takes longer, because the kits can't leave until they're old enough to follow her.

## Can a raccoon be removed in winter?

Yes. The method is the same one used the rest of the year: a one-way door over the opening, so the raccoon can leave and can't get back in, and then the hole is sealed.

Winter adds one wrinkle. Because a raccoon may not come out for days in a hard freeze, the door can't be judged after one night. It stays on until the raccoon has actually left.

Two things not to do:

- Don't seal the hole on a quiet day. In winter, quiet usually means asleep. A raccoon shut in an attic will tear its way out, and it has the strength to come through a ceiling.
- Don't count on trapping it and driving it somewhere. Ontario's rule is that a trapped wild animal can't be let go more than a kilometre from the spot it was trapped, and a raccoon let go that close walks back.

The things the City suggests, like a bright light, a radio and vinegar-soaked rags by the den, work sometimes. In midwinter the raccoon has fewer places to go, so it tends to put up with more. And if it does leave, the hole is still there for the next one.

## Is it kinder to leave it until spring?

It feels that way, but it usually works out worse for both of you. By spring there are kits, and then nothing can be closed until they can travel. In the meantime the insulation is being flattened and soiled, and the opening is letting meltwater in.

A raccoon that loses an attic in January isn't left with nowhere to go. Raccoons keep several dens and move between them. What it loses is the one inside your house.
