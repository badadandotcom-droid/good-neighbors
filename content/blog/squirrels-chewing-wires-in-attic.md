Yes, squirrels chew electrical wires, and in an attic it's a fire risk. They strip the plastic coating off the cable, and a bare wire sitting in dry insulation or against a rafter can arc and start a fire. If you've had squirrels up there, the wiring needs a look from an electrician, and the squirrels need to be out for good before that repair means anything.

## Why squirrels chew on wires

A squirrel's front teeth never stop growing, so it gnaws to keep them worn down. It isn't eating the wire. Cable is about the right thickness to get its teeth around, and in most attics the cables run across the tops of the joists, right along the paths a squirrel uses to get from the hole to its nest.

Wildlife agencies say the same thing wherever grey squirrels live. Nova Scotia's government page on squirrels lists wood and electrical wiring among the things they gnaw. Penn State's extension service puts it plainly: once squirrels have a nest in a building they chew the coating around the wires, and the bare wire can start a fire.

## How a chewed wire starts a fire

House wiring is copper inside a plastic jacket. The jacket keeps the live wire from touching anything. Once it's chewed through, three things can go wrong:

- The bare copper touches another wire or a metal box and shorts out.
- It arcs, which means electricity jumps a small gap and makes a lot of heat in one spot.
- It sits against something that burns. An attic is full of that: dry wood, paper-backed insulation, and the nest itself, which is leaves, shredded paper and pulled-up insulation.

Ontario's fire safety guide lists electrical equipment among the top causes of home fires in the province. Nobody can tell you the odds for your house. What we can say is that a chewed cable doesn't heal, and it stays a risk for as long as it's live.

## Warning signs inside the house

Most chewed wiring gives no sign at all. The cable is behind a ceiling, and it can work normally for months with the jacket gone. So if squirrels have been in the attic, don't wait for a sign.

When there are signs, these are the usual ones:

- Lights that flicker, dim or surge. Ontario's Electrical Safety Authority tells homeowners to call a Licensed Electrical Contractor when they notice this.
- A breaker that keeps tripping, especially one that feeds the upstairs ceiling lights or the bathroom fan.
- A light, fan or outlet that has stopped working for no clear reason.
- A burning smell, or a switch or outlet cover that feels warm.

A burning smell is different from the rest. If you smell burning or see smoke, get everyone out and call 911. Don't go into the attic to look.

## What to do if you find chewed wiring

If you or someone else has been up there and seen cable with the coating chewed off:

- Don't touch it, and don't wrap it in tape. Tape isn't a repair.
- If you know which breaker feeds it, turn that breaker off. If you don't know, leave the panel alone and call an electrician.
- Take a photo from where you're standing, if you can do it without stepping off the joists.
- Call an electrician and tell them animals have been in the attic, so they check the whole run and not one spot.

In Ontario, electrical work is done by a Licensed Electrical Contractor, and the Province's guide says to hire one for all electrical work. They cut out the damaged section and replace it properly.

## Does the damage stop once they're gone?

The chewing stops. The damage that's already done doesn't go anywhere. That's why "we haven't heard them in weeks" isn't a reason to skip the electrician. A cable chewed in October is the same cable in February, with the furnace running and every light in the house on.

It's also why speed matters while they're still up there. Every week a squirrel lives in the attic is another week of gnawing, and it doesn't pick one cable and stay with it.

## Can I cover the wires so they can't chew them?

Not in a way that's worth doing. You'd have to protect every cable in the attic, and the squirrel would still be chewing the rafters, the vent screens and the edge of the hole it came in through. The fix that works is keeping squirrels out of the attic. Steel mesh and sheet metal over the weak spots do that, because squirrels can't chew through steel. The City of Toronto recommends the same materials for covering an opening.
