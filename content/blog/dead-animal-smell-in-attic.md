A heavy, rotten smell that seems to come from the ceiling, or one wall, or one corner of a room is very often a dead animal in the attic or the wall. It's the smell of the body breaking down. It won't go away fast, and a can of air freshener won't fix it. Here's how to tell what you're dealing with, what to leave alone, and what to do next.

## What does a dead animal in the attic smell like?

It smells like rotting meat, and it's usually strongest in the room closest to the spot. It often starts faint and gets stronger over several days. Heat speeds it up, so a warm attic makes it worse.

A smell on its own is a clue, not proof. The only way to know for sure is to find where it's coming from, and that's worth doing before you decide to wait it out.

## Could it be something else?

Yes, and one of them can't wait. If there's any chance the smell is gas, don't go looking for a dead animal. Leave the house first, then call 911 or your gas company from outside. Enbridge Gas and the City of Toronto both give that advice, and Enbridge adds not to use light switches or phones inside, and not to try to find the leak yourself.

A smell that comes up from a sink, a floor drain or a bathroom you don't use much is more likely a drain problem than an animal. The City of Toronto points to drains as the usual source of a sewer smell indoors.

## Other signs it's a dead animal

- Flies collecting in one room, or buzzing at one window, when there's no reason for them to be there.
- Maggots turning up near the spot.
- A damp patch or a stain on the ceiling or a wall right below where the smell is strongest.
- The smell is strongest in one place and fades as you walk away from it.

If you heard scratching or running in the attic or a wall a little while before the smell started, and then it stopped, that's worth mentioning when you call. It's a useful clue, though an animal that goes quiet may also have simply left.

## How did it die up there?

Often the answer is poison. Rodent bait doesn't kill right away, and an animal that eats it can crawl back into a wall or the attic before it dies. The Internet Center for Wildlife Damage Management and Orkin both point to poison as a common reason animals die inside a house.

The other common one is a hole closed too soon. The City of Toronto warns never to seal an opening while an animal could still be inside, because it will die there. That's why nothing should be closed up until you know the space is empty.

## How long does the smell last?

It depends on the size of the animal, the heat, the humidity and how much air moves through the space. For a mouse it's often a week or two. For something bigger, like a raccoon, it can be several weeks. The smell fades as the body dries out, but in a warm attic or a closed wall that can take a long time.

Even after the animal is out, the smell can take a while to clear if fluid soaked into insulation, wood or drywall around it. That's why the spot it was lying on matters as much as the animal itself.

## Should I go up and remove it myself?

You can, but go carefully, and don't start cutting holes. A smell travels, and opening a wall in the wrong place often means opening a second one. Wildlife control sources warn against cutting blindly for exactly this reason.

If you do handle it yourself, follow the province's advice for dead wild animals:

- Never touch it with bare hands. Wear thick gloves, and eye protection if anything could splash.
- Use a doubled plastic bag and tie it off.
- Wear a mask up there, and don't sweep dry droppings or kick up dust.
- Wash your hands for at least 20 seconds afterward, and clean any tools you used.

Ontario's guidance lists several diseases a dead wild animal can carry, including rabies, so this isn't one to rush. If it's a bat, don't touch it at all. In Toronto, a dead bat inside the house goes to Toronto Animal Services through 311.

## Who removes a dead animal in Toronto?

Inside your house, it's yours to deal with. Ontario says the owner is responsible for a dead animal on their own property, and Peel Region says the same and suggests hiring a private company if you'd rather not handle it. The City of Toronto picks up larger dead animals from City property on a 311 request. A dead animal on the road, a sidewalk or in a park is for the City: in Toronto, call 311.
