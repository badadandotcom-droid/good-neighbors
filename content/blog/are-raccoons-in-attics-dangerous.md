A raccoon in the attic is a real problem, but it's rarely a danger to the people in the house if everyone keeps their distance. Three risks are worth knowing. The droppings can carry a parasite. A cornered raccoon will defend itself. And the damage to the house is real. Rabies is the one people worry about most, and in Ontario it's now rare in raccoons. Here's each risk in plain words, and what to do about it.

## Raccoon roundworm in the droppings

Raccoons can carry a roundworm, and its eggs pass out in their droppings. People can be infected by swallowing the eggs, for example from hands that touched soil or droppings. Fresh droppings aren't the problem at first: Manitoba's raccoon fact sheet notes the eggs take a few weeks to become infective.

Human cases are very rare. The BC Centre for Disease Control reports only a handful of known cases in that province, and the University of Saskatchewan's vet college counts very few across North America. The people most at risk are young children and anyone who puts their hands or objects in their mouth.

What makes the droppings worth taking seriously is how long the eggs last. They can survive for years, and Manitoba and WildSafeBC both note that most chemical disinfectants don't kill them. So the goal is to keep the droppings away from people, not to try to wipe them clean.

## How to handle droppings safely

If you have to deal with a small amount yourself, the public-health advice is consistent:

- Wear disposable gloves and boots or shoe covers. In a closed space like an attic, wear an N95 mask.
- Don't sweep droppings dry, which raises dust. Mist them lightly with water first.
- Lift them into a plastic bag, seal it and put it in the garbage.
- Wash your hands well, and wash the clothes you wore in hot water.

Raccoons tend to use the same spot again and again, so an attic can end up with a lot more than a small amount. That's a bigger job. See [raccoon poop cleanup](/wildlife/raccoon-feces-removal).

## Do raccoons in Ontario have rabies?

Raccoons are one of the animals that can carry rabies in Ontario, along with bats, skunks and foxes. Rabies in raccoons is now rare here. An outbreak of the raccoon strain started near Hamilton in 2015. The province reports that strain has been eliminated from Southern Ontario, and its case counts show no raccoon-strain cases in 2024 or 2025.

That doesn't mean ignoring it. If anyone is bitten or scratched by a raccoon, Halton Region and the Simcoe Muskoka health unit give the same steps: wash the wound right away with soap and water, see a doctor, and report it to public health. Toronto's City pages add a call to 311.

A raccoon that's wobbling, seems lost or is acting strangely may be sick with distemper, a virus common in raccoons. Toronto Animal Services has said distemper poses no risk to people. It can spread to dogs that aren't vaccinated, so keep pets away.

## Will a raccoon attack me?

Usually not. Raccoons tend to avoid people. WildSafeBC and Manitoba both note that a raccoon will defend itself when it's cornered or handled, and that a mother is very protective of her kits. The danger comes from trying to get close.

So don't go into the attic to chase it out, don't reach into the hole, and don't try to catch it. Leave it a clear way out, keep children and pets away from where it comes and goes, and call for help.

## What a raccoon does to the house

This is the damage most people end up dealing with. British Columbia's and Nova Scotia's government pages both describe raccoons tearing off shingles, fascia or trim boards to get in. Roof vents and soffits are other common targets. Once inside, a raccoon flattens and soils the insulation where it nests and where it uses its toilet spot.

The hole itself causes trouble too. An opening in the roof lets rain and snow in as well as animals, and it's an open door for the next raccoon or squirrel once this one is gone.

## Are squirrels in the attic dangerous too?

Less so for people. Squirrels seldom carry rabies, as the Simcoe Muskoka health unit and the Canadian Veterinary Medical Association both note. The risk with squirrels is mostly to the house. They chew constantly, and Nova Scotia's government page notes they may chew on wood and electrical wiring. Any chewed wiring should be looked at by an electrician.

Squirrels also widen small openings by gnawing, so a gap that starts small rarely stays that way. Our post on [how to remove squirrels](/blog/how-to-remove-squirrels) covers that job.

## What's the safest thing to do?

- Keep everyone out of the attic until the animal is gone.
- Don't seal the hole yet. A raccoon shut inside will tear its way back out or die up there, and a mother shut out will tear at the roof to get back to her kits.
- Don't touch droppings with bare hands.
- Note where you've seen it going in and out, and take a photo if you can do it safely from the ground.
