The clearest signs of squirrels in an attic are quick running and scratching overhead in the morning and late afternoon, a chewed hole near the roof edge, and squirrels going on and off the roof at the same spot. Raccoons leave bigger signs: heavier noise after dark, torn vents or soffits, and droppings in one spot. Here's what to listen for, what to look for outside and inside, and how to tell whether a hole is still being used.

## What you hear

The time of day is the first clue. The City of Toronto's guide to the city's mammals says grey squirrels are busiest in the early morning and late afternoon. Raccoons are mostly out at night, as the City of Toronto and the City of Mississauga both note.

The weight of the sound is the second. Squirrels usually sound light and quick: scurrying, scratching and gnawing. A raccoon is heavier and slower, with thumps and sometimes a growl or chatter. High-pitched chirping or squeaking can be babies of either kind, often loudest when the mother comes and goes. Our post on [what animal is in your attic](/blog/what-animal-is-in-my-attic) goes through the sounds in more detail.

## Signs on the outside of the house

Most of the evidence is outside, and a lot of it can be seen from the ground. Look for:

- A hole at the roof edge, a soffit corner or around a roof vent. A squirrel's is usually small and chewed round. A raccoon's is bigger, with torn or bent edges.
- Roof vents that are torn, lifted or sitting crooked.
- Soffit panels that have dropped or pulled away, or fascia that looks chewed.
- Loose or lifted shingles near the edge of the roof.
- Squirrels or a raccoon using the same route onto the roof, often a branch, a downspout or a wire.
- Tracks on the roof or in the snow by the house in winter.

The City of Toronto names loose roof vents, rotten or loose soffits, loose shingles and chimneys with no cap among the usual ways in. Branches that hang over the roof are often how the animal gets up there in the first place. Our post on [how raccoons get in the attic](/blog/how-do-raccoons-get-in-the-attic) covers each of these spots.

## Signs inside the attic

If you can get into the attic safely, a few things point to an animal living there:

- Droppings. Squirrel droppings are small, dark pellets, a bit like grains of rice, scattered along the paths they use. Raccoon droppings are much larger, more like a small dog's, and raccoons tend to go in the same spot again and again.
- Nesting. Squirrels pull insulation, leaves and bits of debris into a nest, usually near the roof edge where they came in. Raccoons dig up and flatten insulation over a larger area.
- Chewing. Squirrels gnaw on wood, and sometimes on wires and vent screens.
- Daylight. A spot where light shows through from outside can be the hole itself.

The City of Toronto's advice is not to go into an attic if the floor isn't safe to stand on. A flashlight from the hatch is often enough.

## Is the hole still being used?

The City of Toronto has an easy check for this. Stuff a loose ball of newspaper into the hole and leave it. If it's been pushed out or pulled in, something is using the hole. Toronto Wildlife Centre suggests giving it about three days and nights, and longer in cold or stormy weather, since animals don't always go out every day.

The test has one big limit. Babies too young to leave the nest won't move the paper. Toronto Wildlife Centre's advice is to assume an adult using a hole in spring or summer has young with it. A paper that stays put means no adult has been coming and going. It doesn't mean the attic is empty.

## When are squirrels and raccoons most likely to move in?

Fall is a busy time for squirrels. The City of Toronto's mammal guide says grey squirrels build their winter nests around October, and a warm attic is an easy place to do it. Squirrels have their young twice a year. The City of Mississauga puts the births in March and April and again in July and August.

Raccoons have their kits in the spring. Toronto Wildlife Centre's advice is to assume any raccoon in an attic from January to September could be a mother with young. So for most of the year, a sign in the attic may mean a family, not one animal.

## Safety if you go looking

Keep it simple and keep your distance:

- Don't reach into a hole or a nest. A mother squirrel guarding her nest may act defensively, as both Toronto and Mississauga point out.
- Leave a raccoon a clear way out, and don't try to handle one.
- If you find raccoon droppings, don't touch them with bare hands. The City of Toronto's advice is gloves and a face mask, and washing your hands well after. Our post on whether [raccoons in the attic are dangerous](/blog/are-raccoons-in-attics-dangerous) explains why.
- Don't close up a hole you've found until you know every animal is out.
