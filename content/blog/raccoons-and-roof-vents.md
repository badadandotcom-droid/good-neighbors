A roof vent is a hole cut through the roof with a cover over it. The cover keeps rain out. It was never built to keep a raccoon out, and on a lot of houses it's thin plastic. That makes roof vents one of the most common ways raccoons and squirrels get into an attic, and one of the simplest to fix properly.

## What a roof vent is for

An attic needs air moving through it. Air comes in low, at the soffits, and leaves high, through the vents near the top of the roof. That flow carries out heat in summer and moisture in winter.

To make a vent, the roofer cuts an opening in the roof boards and fastens a cover over it, with the shingles lapped around its base. Most houses have several. From the ground they look like low square boxes on the back slope of the roof.

There are other openings up there as well: the plumbing pipe that sticks up through the roof with a rubber collar around it, and the outlets for bathroom fans and the kitchen exhaust.

## Why animals go for them

Warm air comes out of a roof vent all winter. An animal on the roof can feel it. To a raccoon looking for a den, that's a sign of a warm, dry space a few inches away.

Then it's a matter of strength. A raccoon grips the cover and pulls. A plastic cover that has been through a few winters of sun and frost goes brittle, and it cracks or tears off its base. Thin aluminum bends. Under the cover there's nothing but the opening.

Squirrels do it with their teeth. They chew through the plastic, or through the light screen inside the vent, and they need a much smaller hole.

## Signs a vent has been opened

From the ground, with binoculars if you have them:

- A vent cover that sits crooked, has a corner lifted or is missing.
- A jagged hole in the top or side of a plastic cover.
- Pieces of black or grey plastic in the eavestrough or on the ground.
- Shingles lifted or torn around the base of a vent.
- Insulation or nesting sticking out.

From inside, a torn vent often shows as a patch of daylight in the attic, or as a water stain on the ceiling after rain. An open vent is a hole pointing at the sky. Our post on [roof leaks from animal damage](/blog/roof-leaks-from-animal-damage) covers what the water does.

## Why swapping the cover isn't enough

Putting a new plastic cover on the same opening fixes the leak. It doesn't change anything for the raccoon, which now knows where the attic is. Raccoons often go back to dens they've used, and a new cover of the same kind comes off the same way.

Cornell University's guide to animal-proofing buildings says to replace damaged and vulnerable vents with designs that resist animal entry, and that roof vents should be metal or heavy-duty plastic.

## What a proper vent guard is

A guard is a cage of heavy steel mesh that sits over the vent. The vent keeps working, and the animal can't reach the cover to pull on it.

A good one has a few things going for it.

- Steel an animal can't chew or bend. The guards made for this are heavy expanded steel mesh, galvanized or stainless.
- A flange all the way around, screwed through the shingles into the roof boards, with every screw hole sealed so it doesn't leak.
- Enough height that the mesh stands clear of the vent and doesn't choke the airflow.
- A finish that doesn't rust and streak the shingles. Black blends in on most roofs.

The City of Toronto's raccoon advice says the same in fewer words: use heavy, rust-proof screening to cover open air vents.

If the cover is already torn, the vent is replaced first and the guard goes over the new one. If the roof boards around the opening were chewed or pulled, those are repaired before either goes on.

## Never seal a vent shut

It's tempting to cover a damaged vent with a square of sheet metal or plywood and be done with it. Don't. That vent is part of how the attic breathes. Close one or two and the moisture that used to leave stays in, and over a couple of winters that shows up as damp insulation and wet wood.

The same goes for bathroom and dryer outlets. Those get a cover or screen made for the job, and a dryer outlet needs one that doesn't trap lint.

## The other vents on the roof

The rubber collar around the plumbing pipe is a squirrel favourite. A chewed collar is a small hole and a slow leak. The collar is replaced, and a metal guard can go over it.

Vents on the wall of the house, for the dryer and bathroom fans, are where starlings and sparrows nest. That's a different job, and our [bird removal](/wildlife/birds) page explains it.

## Check for animals before anything is covered

A guard over a vent with an animal inside shuts it in. In spring and summer there may be babies in the insulation right under the vent. Every vent that's been opened gets checked from inside before it's covered.
