Removing a raccoon from an attic for good comes down to three things. Find out whether there are babies up there. Get every raccoon out, the mother and her kits together. Then close the way in with something a raccoon can't tear open. Trapping it and driving it across town isn't allowed in Ontario, and sealing the hole while a raccoon is still inside makes a bigger problem than the one you started with. Here's how it works, in order.

## First, make sure it's a raccoon

Raccoons are mostly out at night and asleep during the day, as both the City of Toronto and the Ontario SPCA note. In an attic that usually sounds like heavy, slow thumping and scratching after dark. A squirrel is lighter and quicker and keeps daytime hours. If you're not sure which one you have, our post on [what animal is in your attic](/blog/what-animal-is-in-my-attic) goes through the sounds. If it turns out to be a squirrel, [how to remove squirrels](/blog/how-to-remove-squirrels) covers that job.

High-pitched chirping or crying from the attic is a different clue. Raccoon kits make that sound, and people often take it for birds.

## Why kits change everything

Raccoons in Ontario usually have their young in spring, most often in April or May, though the Ontario SPCA notes births can run from March into September. The kits stay in the den for about eight weeks before they're big enough to go out with their mother. Toronto Wildlife Centre's advice is to assume that any raccoon in an attic between January and September could be a mother with young.

A mother raccoon keeps more than one den nearby. When she's disturbed, she may take a few nights to carry her kits to another one.

This is where rushing goes wrong. Close the hole while the kits are inside and the mother is shut out. She will work hard to get back to them, often by tearing at the roof. The kits, left behind, can starve up there.

## The City's light, radio and vinegar method

The City of Toronto suggests a gentle first try to make the attic an unpleasant place to live: a bright light, a radio playing talk, and rags soaked in vinegar near the den. Toronto Wildlife Centre adds that it should run for at least three days before you check whether the raccoon has gone.

It works sometimes. But it can't tell you whether kits are up there, and the City itself warns that vinegar fumes can harm young that can't get away. And when the raccoon does leave, the hole is still there. Raccoons come back to it, and so does the next one.

## Mothballs and repellents

Mothballs are a pesticide for clothes moths, not raccoons. Health Canada approves them only for use indoors, sealed in an airtight container. Store-bought repellents run into the same wall as the vinegar and the radio: none of them closes the hole.

## Can I trap a raccoon and move it?

Not the way most people mean it. Under Ontario's rules for wildlife on private property, an animal you catch alive must be let go within 24 hours, no more than a kilometre from where you caught it. Toronto's own trapping page gives the same limits.

One kilometre is a few streets. A raccoon let go that close can find its way back, and the hole in your roof is still open. The City points out a second problem with trapping: a mother caught in a trap leaves her kits behind.

## Poison isn't an option

The City of Toronto warns that using poison on wildlife can lead to criminal or provincial charges. It's a bad idea on its own terms too. A poisoned raccoon can die somewhere nobody can reach, and any kits are left without her.

## What a one-way door does

A one-way door is a hinged door fitted over the hole the raccoon is using. The raccoon can push its way out. Coming back the other way, the door stays shut. Toronto Wildlife Centre lists one-way doors among the preferred tools for a raccoon in the attic.

It only works in the right order. The other gaps on the house get closed first, so the door covers the one way out. And it only goes on once every raccoon up there can use it. A door over the hole while the kits are too young to follow their mother is the same as sealing them in.

## Seal it with something a raccoon can't tear

Raccoons are strong. The City of Mississauga notes that they tear into roof vents, exhaust vents, soffits and chimneys. The Ontario SPCA points out that plastic put over an opening is easily torn away.

So the repair matters as much as the removal. The City of Toronto recommends galvanized sheet metal and rust-proof screening, and the Ontario SPCA names galvanized steel screen. The City also makes the point that a hole left open is a way in for the next animal.

The City's list of the usual ways in: uncapped chimneys, loose shingles, and gaps in roofs, attics and eaves. On a lot of houses here, that means a roof vent, a soffit corner or the spot where the eavestrough meets the roofline. Never close one until you know every raccoon is out. A raccoon sealed inside either tears its way back out or dies up there.

## Should I do it myself?

The City of Toronto leaves the choice to you: handle it yourself within the rules, or hire someone to act for you. The province's page on wildlife damaging private property also points to wildlife removal businesses. What makes a raccoon job hard is the combination: working at roof height around a strong animal, knowing for sure whether there are kits, and making a repair it can't pull back open.
