You can remove a squirrel nest from your attic, but not while squirrels are still using it. The nest is the easy part. What matters is who's in it. If there are babies, they have to leave with their mother first. If there aren't, the adults go out through a one-way door, the hole is sealed, and then the nest material comes out. Here's how to tell which situation you have.

## What a squirrel nest looks like

In a tree, a grey squirrel builds a ball of dry leaves and twigs in the fork of a branch. Canada's Hinterland Who's Who calls it a drey, and you can spot them all over Toronto once the leaves are down.

In an attic the squirrel uses what's there. You'll usually find a hollowed-out mound of insulation mixed with leaves, twigs, shredded paper, bits of cardboard and sometimes fabric. A mother lines it with her own fur. Toronto's and Mississauga's wildlife pages both note that a nesting female loses hair on her shoulders and back for that reason, so a squirrel with bald patches on your roof is a clue.

The nest is usually close to the roof edge, within a metre or two of the hole they come in by. Toronto's guide to its mammals adds that grey squirrels will nest inside the outside walls of houses, so a nest isn't always where you can see it from the hatch.

## Is anything living in it?

Start with the calendar. The City of Mississauga has grey squirrels giving birth in two stretches, March to April and July to August, with the young weaned at about eight weeks. Hinterland Who's Who says young squirrels start making short trips out of the nest at around eight weeks too. So from about March to June, and again from July into October, a nest in use may have babies in it.

Then listen and watch:

- Chirping or thin squeaking from one spot, often when the mother comes back.
- An adult using one hole over and over through the day.
- Fresh material. New leaves and clean, fluffed-up insulation mean it's in use. A flat, dusty nest with old droppings is more likely abandoned.

Winter nests are different. Squirrels don't hibernate, and in cold weather several adults may share one nest for warmth. A winter nest usually has no babies, but it does have squirrels.

If you can't tell, treat it as occupied.

## Can I take the nest out myself?

Once it's empty, yes. It's leaves and insulation. Wear gloves and a mask, bag it, and put it in the garbage.

While it's in use, don't. Three things go wrong:

- You pull out the nest and the babies are in it. Now you have newborn squirrels in a garbage bag, and a mother who will tear into the attic to find them.
- You pull out the nest, the adults leave, and you close the hole. If babies were tucked deeper in the insulation than you looked, they're shut in.
- You pull out the nest and leave the hole. The squirrels build another one, usually by the next day.

There's also the mother herself. The City of Toronto warns that a squirrel protecting a nest can turn defensive, and that you should never grab one, because it can bite through gloves.

## What about just moving the squirrels?

Ontario doesn't let you take a squirrel away and drop it somewhere else. The City of Mississauga points to the Province's rule that wildlife can't be released more than one kilometre from where it was caught. A squirrel let go inside a kilometre comes back to the hole, and one trapped while she has babies leaves the babies behind.

The City of Toronto's own method is to make the attic unpleasant with noise, a talk radio station and bright light, then check whether they've gone. It works sometimes. The hole hasn't gone anywhere, though, and an empty attic with a way in doesn't stay empty.

## How the nest comes out properly

The order is what makes it work.

1. Look for the other openings. A squirrel that loses one door will use another, so the whole roofline gets checked.
2. Check the nest for babies before touching the hole.
3. If there are babies, they go out with their mother. We lift each one out and settle them in a warm box outdoors, right by the opening, so she can carry them off to another nest. Squirrels keep more than one.
4. A one-way door goes over the main hole. Adults can push out and can't push back in.
5. Once they're out, the door comes off and the opening is closed with steel mesh or sheet metal, then the soffit, fascia or vent is repaired.
6. The nest material and droppings are cleaned out.

Step 6 is last on purpose. Taking the nest out first just makes them rebuild.

## Is there a best time of year to do it?

There are easier times. Late fall and winter, and a few weeks in early summer between litters, are less likely to involve babies, so the job can go faster.

But don't wait for one. A squirrel in the attic in April is chewing in April. Wood, vent screens and wiring all get gnawed, and our post on [squirrels chewing wires](/blog/squirrels-chewing-wires-in-attic) explains why that one can't sit. The job can be done in any month. In baby season it takes the extra step with the box.

## Does the old nest need to come out?

It should. Old nest material holds droppings and smells like squirrel, and it's flattened insulation that isn't keeping heat in any more. It's also dry, loose and sitting right where the cables run.

It isn't urgent the way the hole is. Close the house first. The cleanup can follow.

## Will they come back to the same spot?

They'll try. Grey squirrels live in the same small area for years, and one that was raised in your attic knows the way up. That's why the material matters more than the patch. Squirrels chew through wood, plastic, foam and thin aluminum. They don't chew through steel. The City of Toronto's advice is hardware cloth or sheet metal that extends at least 15 cm past the hole on every side.

Trimming branches back from the roof helps too. It doesn't stop a squirrel, since they climb brick, but it takes away the easy route.
