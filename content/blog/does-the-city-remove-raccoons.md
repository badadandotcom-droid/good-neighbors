The short answer is no. In Toronto, and in most of the GTA, the City doesn't come and take a healthy raccoon out of your attic or chimney. Toronto Animal Services steps in when a raccoon is sick, injured or in distress. A healthy raccoon living in your house is the homeowner's to deal with, either yourself within Ontario's rules, or by hiring someone. Here's what the City does do, what the other cities and towns around Toronto say, and what your options are.

## What does Toronto Animal Services do about raccoons?

Toronto Animal Services responds to wildlife that is sick, injured or distressed, and the City names raccoons as an example. You report it through 311, and an officer comes out to check on the animal and pick it up if needed.

What the City doesn't do is remove a healthy raccoon from a home. Its wildlife trapping page says City staff don't trap wildlife for homeowners and don't lend or rent traps. It tells homeowners to handle it themselves within the rules or hire someone to act for them. The City's raccoon page gives homeowners their own steps for moving a raccoon out and closing up the house.

## Will animal control remove a raccoon from my attic?

Not in Toronto, and not in most of the GTA. Here's what the cities and towns say on their own pages:

- Mississauga's Animal Services won't respond to calls to remove healthy wildlife, and suggests a removal company as a last resort after you've tried to move the animal on.
- Brampton's Animal Services doesn't remove wildlife from homes and points residents to a wildlife removal company.
- Aurora's Animal Services deals with injured wildlife on Town property. For wildlife in a home, the Town says to hire a local removal company.
- Ajax handles wildlife only when it's injured or on Town property.
- Whitby doesn't trap or relocate nuisance wildlife, and gives advice on keeping animals out instead.
- Pickering says it doesn't respond directly to many wildlife concerns, and suggests a private company if the problem goes on.

## Does animal control charge to remove a raccoon?

None of the city pages we read lists a fee, because none of them offers the service for a healthy raccoon in a house. The province's advice is to hire a wildlife agent yourself. Ontario's ministry gives advice only and doesn't come out to deal with wildlife on your property.

## When should I call 311 about a raccoon?

Call 311 in Toronto when a raccoon looks sick or hurt. The City's list of warning signs includes a raccoon that's out in the day and acting strangely, crusty or sunken eyes, stumbling or seeming lost, laboured breathing, seizures and unusual aggression. Toronto Wildlife Centre adds that a raccoon out in daylight that's alert and acting normally is usually fine. A mother raising kits, for one, sometimes has to forage in the day.

Don't touch or feed a raccoon that looks sick, and keep your pets away from it. Distemper, a virus common in raccoons, can't spread to people, but it can spread to dogs that aren't vaccinated. If a person or a pet is bitten or scratched, wash the wound well with soap and water and get medical care. In Toronto, the City also asks you to call 311. Peel Region and York Region public health give the same first steps.

## What about a dead raccoon?

A dead raccoon on a road, a sidewalk or in a park is for the City: in Toronto, report it to 311 and the City picks it up. A dead raccoon on your own property, in the attic, in a wall or under the deck, is a different job. That's [dead animal removal](/wildlife/dead-animal-removal), and we do it.

## Can I trap a raccoon and move it myself?

Ontario lets a property owner capture a wild animal that's damaging their property, with limits. It has to be released within 24 hours and within one kilometre of where it was caught. Toronto's trapping page sets out the same limits. The province's own page notes that moving an animal often doesn't solve the problem. One kilometre is a few streets, close enough for a raccoon to find its way back to an open hole.

Poison and glue traps are banned for wildlife in Ontario, and the City of Toronto warns that using poison or body-gripping traps can lead to charges.

Trapping in spring and summer has one more problem. A mother caught in a trap leaves her kits behind. The City's raccoon page says to make sure every raccoon is out before anything is sealed, especially from March to July.

## What to ask a wildlife removal company

Since the City won't come for a healthy raccoon, you'll be choosing a company. The advice from the province and from Toronto Wildlife Centre comes down to a few questions:

- Will someone inspect the house and explain the plan and the price before any work starts?
- Do they check for babies, whatever the time of year?
- Do they use one-way doors and keep the family together, rather than moving raccoons away?
- Do they seal the way in, and will they stand behind that repair in writing?
- Do they carry liability insurance?
- Can they give you references?
