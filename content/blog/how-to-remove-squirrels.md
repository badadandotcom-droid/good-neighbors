The way to remove squirrels from an attic for good has three parts. Make sure there are no babies up there. Put a one-way door over the hole so the squirrel can leave and can't get back in. Then seal the house. Most of what people try first, like mothballs or trapping the squirrel and driving it away, either doesn't work or isn't allowed in Ontario. Here's what people try, what goes wrong, and what works.

## First, make sure it's a squirrel

Squirrels keep daytime hours. The City of Mississauga's wildlife page says they're active during the day and asleep at night. In an attic that sounds like quick running, scratching and chewing, loudest early and late in the day, and mostly quiet after dark. Heavy thumping at night is more likely a raccoon, and that's a different job. If you're not sure, our post on [what animal is in your attic](/blog/what-animal-is-in-my-attic) goes through the sounds.

The black squirrels you see around Toronto are grey squirrels too, with a darker coat. Same animal, same habits.

## Lights, a radio and vinegar

The City of Mississauga suggests a gentle first try. For three days and nights in a row, put a bright light that doesn't give off heat near the nest, play a radio loudly up there, and leave rags soaked in apple cider vinegar nearby. The idea is to make the attic an unpleasant place to live, so the squirrel moves out on its own.

It's worth a try if you know its limits. It can't tell you whether there are babies. And even when the squirrel leaves, the hole is still open. A warm attic with a way in is what the next squirrel is looking for, and in the fall that search is on: grey squirrels build their winter nests around October.

## Mothballs and store-bought repellents

Mothballs are a pesticide for clothes moths, not squirrels. Health Canada's direction is to use them only inside airtight containers. Spread around an attic, the fumes end up in your house.

Store-bought repellents don't do much better. The City of Edmonton's squirrel tip sheet notes that there are no registered squirrel repellents. And no spray or powder closes the hole the squirrel came in through.

## Can I trap a squirrel and move it?

Not the way most people picture it. Ontario's rules for wildlife on private property say a live-caught animal has to be released within 24 hours and within one kilometre of where it was caught. The City of Toronto's trapping page says the same.

One kilometre is a few blocks. The province's reason for the rule is that an animal released close by still knows where its food and dens are. For an attic squirrel, that includes your attic, and the hole is still open.

Trapping has other problems. The City of Edmonton notes that traps set inside an attic don't work, and that trapping can leave babies behind with no mother.

## Don't use poison

Ontario's Fish and Wildlife Conservation Act bans poison for wild animals. It's also a bad idea on its own terms. A poisoned squirrel can die inside a wall or a ceiling where nobody can reach it, and any babies are left up there without her.

## Check for babies first

Grey squirrels have two litters a year. The City of Mississauga says babies are born in March and April and again in July and August, and they're weaned at about eight weeks. The City of Toronto's guide to the city's mammals gives the same pattern, late winter and summer. So for much of spring and summer, a squirrel in the attic may have a nest of babies with her.

This is why the order matters. Shut the mother out and the babies are stuck inside without her. She will work hard to get back to them, often by chewing a new hole somewhere else on the roof.

Outside those months, the squirrel is more likely to be on its own. We still check every time before anything goes on the hole.

## How a one-way door works

A one-way door is a small hinged door or tunnel fitted over the hole the squirrel is using. The squirrel can push its way out when it goes to feed. It can't push its way back in. The City of Edmonton recommends one-way doors over traps for squirrels in an attic.

For it to work, the squirrel needs one way out and only one. So the other gaps on the house get closed first, and the door goes on the main hole. If there are babies, we keep them with their mother. We take them out of the nest by hand and set them in a warm box outside, right beside the hole, so she can find them and carry them to another nest. Nothing is left shut in without her.

## Seal it, or it happens again

Getting the squirrel out is half the job. The City of Edmonton's tip sheet makes the point that taking an animal out without fixing what drew it in just makes room for the next one. Its advice is to seal the holes with steel, which a squirrel can't chew.

The Wisconsin Humane Society lists the usual ways in for squirrels and raccoons: loose, missing or rotten soffit, fascia and roof boards, and attic vents. On a lot of houses here, that means the roof vents, the corners where the soffit meets the roof, and the fascia board behind the eavestrough. A squirrel that finds a soft spot will often chew the rest of the way through.

## Can I do it myself?

Some people do. What makes it hard is the combination: working at roof height, finding every gap and not only the obvious one, knowing whether there are babies, and fitting a door that the squirrel can't work around. Get one of those wrong and you can shut a squirrel inside, or shut babies away from their mother.
