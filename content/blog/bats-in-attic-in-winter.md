Some bats spend the winter inside houses, and in Ontario it's usually the big brown bat. If one turns up in your living room in January, it didn't fly in from outside. It was already in the building, hibernating in the attic or a wall, and it woke up. Bats can't be put out in winter, so the removal itself waits for spring. The inspection and the plan don't have to.

## Which bats winter in houses?

Ontario has eight kinds of bat. Most of them leave for the winter or hibernate in caves and old mines. Ontario Parks lists the big brown bat as the one that also hibernates in buildings, and says it's often found in houses. Saskatchewan's government says the same about a winter bat indoors: it's most likely a big brown bat that has roused from hibernation.

The little brown bat, the one on Ontario's endangered list, uses attics in summer and mostly winters underground.

A hibernating bat tucks into a spot that stays cold but doesn't freeze: deep in the insulation at the eaves, inside a wall cavity, behind a chimney. It can be one bat or a cluster. Health Canada notes that when conditions suit them, bats will roost in a building right through the winter.

## Why a bat shows up in the house in January

Hibernating bats wake up now and then. Penn State's extension service explains that they stir during the winter to move around, or because the spot they picked has become too warm or too cold. A thaw, a cold snap or the furnace being turned up can all do it.

A bat that wakes in an attic sometimes follows warm air the wrong way, down a wall, past a pipe or a light fixture, and into the living space. That's the bat you find on the curtain or circling the hallway.

One bat indoors in winter is a strong sign there's a way in from the attic or the walls, and often that it isn't the only bat.

## What to do with a bat in the house tonight

First, the health part, which comes from the City of Toronto and the Canadian Wildlife Health Cooperative:

- Don't touch it with bare hands.
- If anyone was bitten or scratched, or handled it, wash that skin with soap and water for a full 15 minutes and call a doctor or public health right away.
- Do the same if the bat was in a room with someone asleep, a young child, or anyone who can't tell you whether they were touched. Toronto residents can reach Toronto Public Health through 311.
- Keep pets away from it, and call your vet if a pet had hold of it.

Then the bat. In summer the advice is to open a window and let it leave. In winter that doesn't work. Toronto Wildlife Centre says bats can't survive a winter outdoors, so a bat put out into the cold will likely die.

If there was no contact with anyone, close the door to that room and keep people and pets out. Toronto Wildlife Centre's hotline, 416-631-0662, gives advice on a winter bat and on containing it safely.

## Why bats aren't removed in winter

The rest of the year, bats are moved out with one-way doors. A door goes over each gap they use, they fly out to feed, and they can't get back in.

In winter there's nothing for them to fly out to. There are no insects, and a bat that leaves a warm roost in February uses up the fat it needs to last until spring. Ontario's guide to bats in buildings covers December to the end of March this way: some overwintering bats, big browns in particular, may still be inside, so gaps are sealed completely only when it's certain no bats are in there.

That gives a simple calendar:

- December to the end of March: no one-way doors, and no sealing until the bats are known to be out.
- April and early May: one-way doors can go on.
- Mid-May to mid-August: nothing, because the pups can't fly yet.
- Late August through November: one-way doors again.

Bats are also protected. The City of Toronto's page says it's illegal to kill or harm them, and three of Canada's bat species are listed as endangered. Sealing a wall with bats asleep inside it would do exactly that.

## What can be done now

More than you'd think.

- A first visit confirms that it's bats and not mice or squirrels. Droppings are the usual giveaway. Bat droppings crumble to dust when pressed, and they pile up below the spot where the bats hang.
- The outside of the house gets checked for the gaps bats use. They need very little: about a centimetre, at a soffit, behind a fascia board, at a roof vent or where the chimney meets the roof.
- The one-way doors get booked for April, ahead of pup season. Leave it past mid-May and the next opening is late August.
- Inside, the routes from the attic into the living space can be closed off now, since that shuts no bat out of its roost. Gaps around the attic hatch, pipes and light fixtures are the common ones. That's what stops the next bat from ending up in the bedroom.

That last step is something you can do yourself with weatherstripping and caulk.

## Signs of bats in winter

They're easy to miss, because a hibernating bat does almost nothing.

- Faint scratching or squeaking in a wall or ceiling on a mild day or when the heat comes on.
- Small dark droppings on a windowsill, in the attic below the ridge, or on the ground under a gap.
- A brown stain around a small gap at the roofline.
- A bat in the house, in any month.

Watching the roof at sunset, which works in summer, will likely show nothing in winter. That doesn't mean there are no bats.
