## What changed, in short
- 60 neighbourhood, hamlet and village names are gone from these ten pages: the audit's list found 66 names (425 mentions) on them before and finds 6 names (24 mentions) after, all landscape names the owner chose to keep in the sense he kept them (the Rouge and Highland Creek as creeks, the Scarborough Bluffs, Heart Lake Conservation Area, the Oak Ridges Moraine, plus "Mimico Creek", which the list catches as "Mimico").
- The "Neighbourhoods we cover in X" section is gone from the six city pages in this batch (decision 2). Each page now says "all of X" in its intro and in its first question, and describes the city by its parts: the lakefront, the river valley, the postwar streets, the estate lots, housing eras.
- Toronto hub: names Scarborough, North York, Etobicoke, East York, York and downtown Toronto. York, the former city, is new (decision 3); "downtown Toronto" stays because the live ad text and a live keyword use it.
- Ads-side names kept where they were: East York and downtown Toronto (Toronto hub, raccoon and squirrel pages), Bolton (Caledon page and Peel hub).
- Word counts (whole page, as the crawl counts them): Brampton 1,237 to 1,211; Mississauga 1,217 to 1,198; Scarborough 1,143 to 1,129; North York 1,052 to 1,022; Etobicoke 1,072 to 1,030; Caledon 1,053 to 1,023; Toronto hub 1,546 to 1,519; Peel hub 1,668 to 1,659; raccoons 1,785 to 1,777; squirrels 1,651 to 1,641. All city pages stay well over the 500 words of their own.

## Judgment calls (strike any by number)
1. "Professor's Lake" is dropped from the Brampton page and the Peel hub although decision 1 keeps lakes: in Brampton it is the name of a neighbourhood as much as of the lake, so it reads as a neighbourhood. Say the word and it goes back in as the lake.
2. The first question on each city page is now "Do you cover all of {City}?" (it used to name two neighbourhoods). The answers describe the city's parts and still name the covered neighbours (Caledon and Bolton; Brampton and Etobicoke; North York and East York; and so on).
3. Toronto hub, second question: "Do you cover the Kingsway, York Mills and the Bluffs?" is replaced by "Do you cover East York, York and downtown Toronto?", which is where York gets its mention. York is named three times on the hub in all (hero paragraph, "part by part", and this question). The raccoon and squirrel pages still name East York and downtown as before and do not name York.
4. Mississauga's "old mill village in the northwest" and "the harbour" describe Streetsville and Port Credit without naming them. The Peel hub does the same ("the heritage streets around the harbour and the old mill village upriver").
5. Brampton's "the planned streets east of downtown a generation older" describes Bramalea without naming it (checked earlier: a planned community of the late 1950s and 60s).
6. Scarborough: "a 1960s bungalow in the east end" replaces "in West Hill or Cliffside". Cliffside is in the south, not the east; the sentence now points at the Highland Creek and Rouge side, which the surrounding paragraph is about.
7. North York: the three "problems" paragraphs now run estate streets along the valley; the streets to the north and west (bungalows and rebuilds); the planned communities east of the valley. The old text had the same three groups with names.
8. Etobicoke: the Kingsway, Humber Valley Village and Sunnylea become "the homes along its lower valley" and "the big old homes along the river"; the lakefront and the north end are unnamed. The card line is "The old stone homes along the Humber to the lakefront and the postwar north."
9. Caledon keeps "the Escarpment villages" and "the villages on the Escarpment and the moraine" as descriptions, with no village named except Bolton (allowed).
10. Peel hub, second question: "Do you cover Lorne Park, Castlemore and Bolton?" becomes "Do you cover the lakefront, the estate lots and Bolton?"
11. "Scarborough, North York and Etobicoke each have a page of their own with the neighbourhoods listed" (Toronto hub) and its Peel twin lose "with the neighbourhoods listed", since the lists are gone.
12. Squirrel page: "go neighbourhood by neighbourhood" becomes "go part by part", for the same reason.
13. Code: the city data no longer needs a neighbourhood list; a city that still has one (the ten batch-2 cities) keeps showing the old section until batch 2. The rule in the data file's header comment is the new one.
