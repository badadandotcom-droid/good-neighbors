6. The law. The page says: "Most wild birds, and their nests and eggs while the nest is in use, are protected under Ontario and federal law; some, the chimney swift among them, are protected all year. Pigeons, starlings and house sparrows, the three that nest on and in houses most often, are the usual exceptions." Checked Oct 12: Environment and Climate Change Canada's protected-birds list excludes introduced species, and a federal bird-conservation page names rock pigeon, European starling and house sparrow as the three introduced species; Ontario's "Remove bird nests or eggs" page lists house sparrow (with crow, cowbird, grackle and red-winged blackbird) as needing no approval, the starling appeared on that list only in a copy of the page, and the pigeon's provincial status comes from Ontario Field Ornithologists rather than the ministry page (the two government pages could not be opened from this session, only read through search results). Chimney swift: threatened in Ontario, protected all year, in chimneys from early May to mid-August (Birds Canada, Government of Canada). Written as "the usual exceptions" and "we'll tell you what can be done and when", not as legal advice.
