## The short answer

1. **Almost nobody names these towns when they search.** Over 12 months (Sep 2025 to Aug 2026), searches like "raccoon removal king city" were zero for King City, Nobleton, Schomberg, Uxbridge, Port Perry, Kleinburg, Unionville, Oak Ridges, Lorne Park, Bridle Path, Hoggs Hollow, Courtice, East Gwillimbury, Georgina, Bradford and Orangeville.
   - The only towns with real named searches are:
     - Oshawa, about 80 a month
     - Oakville, about 90
     - Burlington, about 90
     - Milton, about 60
     - Bowmanville, about 30
   - For comparison, Pickering (already planned) is about 70.
2. **People in those towns still search, just without the town name.** They type "raccoon removal", "squirrel in attic" and so on. Google knows where they are. Keyword Planner shows its lowest bucket, about 10 a month per keyword, in every town tested. Pickering shows the same, so this number can't rank the towns. It only says "a few".
3. **So the lever is the location, not keywords.** The existing plain ad groups (Raccoon, Squirrel, Attic, Walls, Birds, Bats) already hold the right keywords. A far town only needs to be added to its campaign's location list, at a lower bid.
4. **Google can't target most rich neighbourhoods by name.**
   - These have no location target: Kleinburg, Woodbridge, Unionville, Oak Ridges, Lorne Park, Keswick, Sutton, Cachet, Mineola, Bridle Path, Hoggs Hollow and Castlemore (the last five checked Oct 11).
   - They are reached through their city (Vaughan, Markham, Richmond Hill, Mississauga, Toronto, Brampton) or through Georgina.
   - King is the exception the other way: Google has one target for the whole township.
5. **Drive times put the rings in a different place than we thought.** From Morningside & Finch, off-peak:
   - Close (16–32 min): Pickering, Markham, Ajax, Stouffville, Whitby, Oshawa, Richmond Hill, Vaughan, Aurora, Courtice.
   - Far (35–47 min): Newmarket, Bowmanville, King City, Uxbridge, Nobleton, Mississauga, Brampton, Port Perry, Caledon (Bolton), Schomberg.
   - The 401 makes Durham closer than it feels. Northern York and Peel are farther.
6. **Incomes don't rule out any candidate.** King has the highest median household income in Ontario ($141,000). Every candidate is above Ontario's median ($91,000) except Oshawa ($86,000). Toronto, the core market, is $84,000. Town medians mix renters and owners, so they only rank towns roughly. The -30% "Lower 50%" household-income setting does the real filtering, one household at a time.

## How the ads reach far towns (the big-advertiser way)

1. **Target by location, not by town keywords.** Add the town to the campaign's locations, set to "presence" (people in or regularly in the area), as every campaign already is. The normal ads then show to people there.
2. **Use a close ring and a far ring.**
   - Close towns (up to about 32 min off-peak from Morningside & Finch) keep their normal bids.
   - Far towns (35 min or more) get the same ads at a lower bid. Google calls this a location bid adjustment, and it is one setting per location.
   - Starting point: **-30%**. It works with Manual CPC.
   - Two exceptions: Oshawa is close but starts at -30% (lowest income; THE RULE starts new towns low). Brampton and Mississauga are far but run live without a location setting, because Peel's bids already start lower; the weekly rule decides.
3. **Let the results decide.** The Locations report in Google Ads shows which town every click and call came from. The weekly rule then moves bids town by town: $100 with no lead means bid down 20%; $150 with no lead means pause; a booked job under $250 means bid up 15–20%.
4. **Pages only where they pay.** Big cities have their own pages. Small towns land on the region hub or the animal pages.

**The gap this closes:** the city ad groups already have their own bids per town. The plain ad groups ("raccoon in attic", "squirrel removal" and so on) bid the same for every town in a campaign. Without the far-ring setting, someone in Newmarket gets the same bid as someone in Markham.

**Note for the ads chat:** location adjustments multiply with the -30% household-income adjustment. A lower-50% household in a far town gets about half the normal bid (0.7 × 0.7 = 0.49).
