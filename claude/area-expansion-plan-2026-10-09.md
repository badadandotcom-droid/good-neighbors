## THE RULE (Duane, Oct 9, 11:48 PM; this overrides everything below)
"I don't need the most leads or clicks. I need the cost to be low and the job to be high. I don't mind missing some leads. Find every single way to reduce the cost per lead. Start low."
- Bids start at the LOW end of the range (position 3–4). Raise a bid only after that keyword, town or hour has produced a booked job at a good cost.
- Be first only where first is cheap (thin auctions). Bid low in expensive towns and take whatever spot that buys. Skip far or expensive towns until the closer ones prove out.
- High-value jobs first: raccoon or squirrel in the attic, roof or walls ($800–1,000+). Inside-the-house keywords are the only ones that bid for first place.
- Weekly: $100 spent on a keyword/town with no lead → lower its bid 20%. $150 with no lead → pause. A booked job at under $250 of spend → raise 15–20%. Move budget to the lowest cost per booked job.
- Search terms checked DAILY for junk once ads are live (Duane, Oct 10). Daily is done in Duane's Chrome; automatic daily checks would need a paid Supermetrics plan.

## Which bid numbers to trust
- Keyword Planner "avg monthly searches" = average of the last 12 months (seasons blended). Its bid range = what advertisers paid for top-of-page spots over recent months. Neither is live.
- The account's own per-keyword estimates (Est. first page bid / Est. top of page bid, in the Keywords table) are refreshed from live auctions and include OUR Quality Score, but for low-volume keywords they are unreliable: on Oct 7–9 we paid within a dollar of our max bid on nearly every click, even where Google estimated $2–4. See claude/competitors-and-live-bids-2026-10-10.md.
- Quality Score drives the price: "squirrel in attic" was 1/10 on Oct 9 (general ad + general page). Give attic/wall keywords their own ad group, an ad that says "attic", and the rewritten /wildlife/something-in-the-attic page; the estimate should fall. Same logic as the city pages.
- Auction insights report: shows which competitors appear on the same searches and how often they outrank us. Closest thing to seeing competitor bids. Oct 3–9 results are in the competitors doc.

## Every lever that cuts cost per lead (in order of impact)
1. Bid low; accept position 3–4. (Oct 3–9 we were absolute top 43% of the time: the premium spot.)
2. Spend on inside-the-house searches ("raccoon in attic", "squirrel in attic", "squirrel in wall") before generic "removal toronto".
3. Town by town: first only where it's cheap; low bids in dear towns; skip far towns.
4. Raise only on proof; cut and pause by the weekly rule above.
5. Landing pages and ads that match the search (the city pages; attic keywords → the rewritten attic page). Better Quality Score = cheaper clicks for the same spot.
6. Google Business Profile: the top of the Google page for "raccoon removal toronto" is the free Businesses block, and we're not in it. Set it up right after the city pages.
7. Google Local Services Ads: pay per lead, not per click; the second thing on the page. Needs a verified Business Profile first.
8. After 2–3 weeks of data: cut hours and devices that click but don't call.
9. Call-focused ads (call asset, "Calls answered 24/7") so people call instead of browsing.
10. Exact match everywhere; phrase match only for city terms. Shared negative list, grown daily. Competitor brand names blocked (done Oct 10).
11. Free channels: the city pages ranking organically, Nextdoor, Facebook, Kijiji.

## Why city pages
- People search by city, not region. "raccoon removal brampton" ~720/month; "raccoon removal peel region" ~0.
- The four existing region pages (/service-areas/toronto, york-region, durham-region, peel-region) are templates. They won't rank and they're weak ad landing pages. Google rated the landing page "below average" on the Brampton keyword.
- Brampton alone has about as many wildlife searches as Toronto, at roughly a third less per click. The one real 60-second call this week came from "raccoon removal brampton".
